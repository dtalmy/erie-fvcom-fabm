Lake Erie hydrodynamic-biogeochemical modeling with FVCOM-FABM

Our goal is to simulate the lower trophic levels and biogeochemistry of Lake Erie under realistic physical transport. We couple the FVCOM hydrodynamic model, which resolves the lake's currents, mixing, temperature and ice, to an ecosystem model built in FABM. The coupled model lets us ask how ecological dynamics and cellular physiological processes influence nutrient and carbon cycling across the lake.

Next steps

We can now use the model to diagnose why the timing of the blooms is off. The one-year simulation took about 30 hours on 64 processors, mostly because of FVCOM's high-resolution physics. Running the biology offline, driven by saved physics, and with a longer time step could make each run much faster. That would let us run more experiments, for example lowering diatom growth in cold water, or changing how resistant Microcystis is to grazing and how much it loses to viral infection, to see what sustains the summer bloom. Further ahead, the model could be extended with a carbonate system and urea, or with a simple representation of light harvesting by rhodopsins, which may matter for diatoms under the ice.
